How this was built
  • Homes. Sample points on a 350 m grid covering today's Duniway, Lewis and Llewellyn elementary attendance areas (City of Portland GIS), which together form the Sellwood MS area. Of the 96 grid points, 29 fell in open-space zones (the Willamette riverbank, Oaks Bottom Wildlife Refuge, Sellwood Riverfront and Westmoreland parks, Eastmoreland Golf Course) and 4 more sat on the river with no street within 100 m; all 33 were removed, leaving 63: 23 in the Llewellyn area and 40 in the Duniway and Lewis areas. Each is labeled by its nearest cross streets. They are evenly spaced locations, not student addresses, and each counts equally.
  • Scenarios. Each sample home is assigned using the district's Scenario A and B grade 6–8 boundaries, simplified to street lines. Scenario A: homes west of SE McLoughlin Blvd go to Hosford MS and homes east of it go to Brentwood MS. Scenario B: Llewellyn-area homes north of SE Tacoma St go to Hosford MS; Llewellyn-area homes south of Tacoma and all Duniway- and Lewis-area homes go to Brentwood MS. Homes right at a boundary street may be assigned differently in the district's final maps.
  • Routes. Walking and driving routes are shortest-time routes on OpenStreetMap from the public OSRM foot and car profiles; walking assumes about 2.9 mph. Bike routes come from the Valhalla bicycle router (OpenStreetMap data, FOSSGIS server) set to strongly prefer bike lanes, neighborhood greenways, multi-use paths and bike/pedestrian bridges such as the Springwater Corridor and the Powell Blvd overcrossing at SE 17th, even when that adds distance. One extra rule: a bike route may cross a High Crash Network street but not ride along it. Where the router chose a stretch of one, that stretch was blocked and the trip re-routed until it stopped changing. Trips to Hosford are also required to use the SE 17th Ave route over the Powell Blvd overcrossing ("The Octopus") and the path beside Powell, the route local riders described; the router plans the rest of each trip. Bike times assume a steady 9 mph. Driving times from the routing engine assume no traffic.
  • Traffic estimates. Drive mode also shows estimates that multiply the no-traffic time by Portland's 2025 TomTom Traffic Index congestion levels: 1.51 for the morning rush and 1.77 for the evening rush (school pickup around 3–4 PM falls in its leading edge). These are citywide averages; McLoughlin, Powell and Holgate may run slower or faster than average, and the estimates leave out drop-off lines and parking.
  • High Crash Network. A route counts if it runs along or crosses one of PBOT's High Crash Network streets (within 20 m).
  • 30+ mph crossings. Places where a route crosses from one side of a street posted at 30 mph or more to the other, merged when closer than 40 m. A crossing counts only if the route is more than 8 m from the street 35 m before and 35 m after it, so riding or walking alongside a street is not counted. Crossings on a bridge or through an underpass (from OpenStreetMap bridge and tunnel tags), such as the Bybee Blvd overpass or the Powell Blvd overcrossing at SE 17th, are not counted because the traveler never meets that traffic. The same tests apply to crossings of High Crash Network streets.
  • Neighborhood streets. The share of each route on streets the City of Portland classifies as local streets, plus off-street paths more than 20 m from any street. Collectors and arterials such as SE 17th, Bybee, Milwaukie, Holgate and Powell do not count, even where they have bike lanes. Each stretch of route is matched to the nearest street within 20 m.
  • Crashes on the route. ODOT-reported injury crashes from 2019–2024 within 25 m of the route. Busier and longer routes collect more crashes, so this measures exposure along the path, not the risk of a single trip.
  • School commute hours. Weekday crashes from 7–9 AM and 2–4 PM, September through June.
  • Traffic counts. The most recent PBOT count since 2017 at each location with at least 3,000 vehicles a day. "Morning peak" is the busiest hour between midnight and noon on the count day.
What this map does not show
  • TriMet bus trips, carpools or any district-provided transportation. The district says transportation for receiving schools still needs implementation planning.
  • Crossing treatments such as signals, beacons and crossing guards, which change how a crossing works in practice.
  • Unreported crashes and near misses.
  • Measured congestion on these particular streets. Drive-time estimates use citywide averages, and the traffic counts show volume, not delay.
  • Benefits the district cites for consolidation, such as enrollment stability and program offerings. Those are covered in the board materials linked below.
